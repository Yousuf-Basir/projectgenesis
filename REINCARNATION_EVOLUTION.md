# Reincarnation selection and inheritance — 2026-10-23

The first four 1,000-tick worlds still sample the original recipes. Later worlds select from the three highest scoring recent results (history limited to 24), with a 25% chance to explore an original recipe. One parameter changes per world: food growth within 0.2–0.5, growth attempts within 4–10, or collective movement multiplier within 0.6–1.1. Search randomness is reproducible from the base seed. Recipe parent and parameter before/after are exposed in the API.

Selection uses a separate bounded score: population retention relative to actual cycle entry (capped at 2), remaining bonds relative to bonds formed (capped at 1), gate completions per final member (capped at 1), and facets per final member (capped at 1). The historical display score is retained. This is a heuristic with environmental confounding: richer worlds may score better. Matched controls are needed before claiming adaptation.

All eligible surviving structures still carry forward. Half of the 24 fresh newcomers are replaced with descendants selected from the top third of carried members by normalized age plus current energy. Their energy and starting sites use the existing newcomer budget. Each descendant has a 25% mutation chance through the existing arithmetic genome mutation engine, a newly generated brain from that genome, and at most 0.2 inherited node trace (10% of parent trace). Connections form through ordinary simulation rules; offspring are not given copied bonds. Parent ranking measures survival and energy, not proof of useful problem solving.

Carried members retain age, use the new world's random stream, and retain bonds with the existing 10% reserve transition loss. Component reserve capture now includes each surviving group's reserve independently. Cycle history records inherited descendant and mutation counts; API `reincarnation.inheritance` reports the current entry cohort. Controller option `evolutionEnabled: false` disables recipe mutation and descendant replacement for comparison.

This makes cumulative genetic selection possible. It does not evolve neural edge weights, guarantee improvement, copy a whole morphology into offspring, or save evolution across process restarts. The initial four recipes remain a curriculum. Next measurement should compare identical seed schedules with evolution enabled/disabled, track lineage survival and gate work, and evaluate finalists on held-out worlds before changing the selection objective.

Validation: `node test/reincarnation-evolution.mjs` runs one actual 1,000-tick reference world and checks descendant replacement, genome mutation, geometry retention, unique IDs, bond endpoints, and variation beyond the four recipes. This verifies operation; long-run improvement requires the matched comparison described above.
